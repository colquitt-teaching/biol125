Population coding of movement direction

A monkey moves a joystick toward a light while we record from upper motor neurons in primary motor cortex. Each neuron is broadly tuned, so on its own it can’t tell you which way the arm moved. Combine many neurons into a population vector and the direction pops out, accurately enough to drive a robotic arm.

Simplified, illustrative model. Each neuron fires at baseline + depth × cos(movement direction − preferred direction), with Poisson spike noise, from about 150 ms before movement onset until the movement ends. Preferred directions are spread evenly at random. The population vector weights each neuron’s preferred direction by its change in firing from baseline, as in Georgopoulos’s experiments.
